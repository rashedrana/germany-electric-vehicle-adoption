Year-on-year sales growth — Germany

The boom (2020: +261%), the subsidy shock (2023: −16%, 2024: −19%) and the rebound (2025: +51%) — the paper's central episode.

The whole car market — Germany (implied)

Total market implied by sales ÷ share. The market itself grew 2022→2024 (+6.4%) — the EV dip was a share collapse, not a shrinking market.

The road to 15 million — Germany's EV fleet (cumulative sales)

Fleet proxied by cumulative sales (the paper's method). Hitting 15m by 2030 needs 2,131,704 sales a year — 2.48× the 2025 pace. At the flat 2025 pace Germany reaches only ~8.6m.

EV share league table — top 12 countries, 2025

Germany's 30.0% ranks only #19 of 59 countries — but its 860,000 cars rank #3 in the world by volume.

Does income explain EV adoption? EV share vs GDP per capita, 59 countries (2022)

Each dot is a country (2022, the paper's regression sample). Richer countries adopt more (slope +10.6 pp per log unit of GDP per capita), but income explains only 19% of the variation (R² = 0.189) — and Germany (purple) sits 11.2 pp above its income-predicted share.
Built by Rashed Rana as the interactive companion to the research paper “From 140 to 860,000: Electric Vehicle Adoption in Germany, 2010–2025 — Growth, the Subsidy Shock, and the European Comparison” — the full analysis, SQL queries and paper live in the GitHub project. This web edition mirrors the Power BI report (star-schema model, DAX measures and page layout in the repo under dashboard/powerbi/). Data: Our World in Data (based on IEA Global EV Outlook) — electric cars are battery-electric and plug-in hybrid cars combined; the source data has no BEV/PHEV split. Total market and fleet figures are derived (sales ÷ share; cumulative sales) exactly as documented in the paper.
