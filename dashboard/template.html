Year-on-year sales growth — Germany

The boom (2020: +261%), the subsidy shock (2023: −16%, 2024: −19%) and the rebound (2025: +51%) — the paper's central episode.

The whole car market — Germany (implied)

Total market implied by sales ÷ share. The market itself grew 2022→2024 (+6.4%) — the EV dip was a share collapse, not a shrinking market.

The road to 15 million — Germany's EV fleet (cumulative sales)

Fleet proxied by cumulative sales (the paper's method). Hitting 15m by 2030 needs 2,131,704 sales a year — 2.48× the 2025 pace. At the flat 2025 pace Germany reaches only ~8.6m.

EV share league table — top 12 countries, 2025

Germany's 30.0% ranks only #19 of 59 countries — but its 860,000 cars rank #3 in the world by volume.

Does income explain EV adoption? EV share vs GDP per capita, 59 countries (2022)

Each dot is a country (2022, the paper's regression sample). Richer countries adopt more (slope +10.6 pp per log unit of GDP per capita), but income explains only 19% of the variation (R² = 0.189) — and Germany (purple) sits 11.2 pp above its income-predicted share.

A decade of catch-up — EV share 2015 vs 2025 (slope chart)

In 2015 only Norway (22.0%) was past take-off — every other peer sat at or below 3.5%. Norway also gained the most (+75.0 pp), ahead of Denmark (+68.7 pp) and Sweden (+58.6 pp). Germany gained +29.3 pp; the United States the least (+9.3 pp).

Out of every 100 new cars — Germany, 2025 (waffle chart)

Each square is one car in 100. In 2015 the same grid would have shown fewer than one filled square (0.7%); even the 2022 peak year filled only 31.

The tipping year clusters on 2020 — Germany, France, the UK, Denmark and the EU27 all first crossed 10% that year. Norway crossed 50% first (2019); by 2025 five of the ten peers sat at 50% or more. The panel median rose from 1.2% (2015) to 44.0% (2025).

Step by step to 15 million — fleet vs the required path (step line)

Each step is one year of sales added to the fleet. The actual staircase reaches 4,341,480 by end-2025 — 28.9% of the target. The required straight-line path (2,131,704 added every year) would need the fleet to almost double by 2027 (8,604,888).

Data source

Our World in Data — electric car sales and market-share data, sourced by OWID from the IEA Global EV Outlook: ourworldindata.org/grapher/electric-car-sales

Coverage: 64 countries/aggregates, 2010–2025 — 2,999 fact rows in the Power BI star schema in this repo (10-country peer panel plus the full 64-entity dataset). Every figure on this page matches the linked scientific paper, “From 140 to 860,000: Electric Vehicle Adoption in Germany, 2010–2025”, number for number.

Electric cars are battery-electric (BEV) and plug-in hybrid (PHEV) cars combined — the source data has no BEV/PHEV split, so no chart here invents one. Cumulative sales (fleet proxy) and the implied total market (sales ÷ share) are derived exactly as documented in the paper.

Built by Rashed Rana as the interactive companion to the research paper “From 140 to 860,000: Electric Vehicle Adoption in Germany, 2010–2025 — Growth, the Subsidy Shock, and the European Comparison” — the full analysis, SQL queries and paper live in the GitHub project. This web edition mirrors the Power BI report (star-schema model, DAX measures and page layout in the repo under dashboard/powerbi/). Data: Our World in Data (based on IEA Global EV Outlook) — electric cars are battery-electric and plug-in hybrid cars combined; the source data has no BEV/PHEV split. Total market and fleet figures are derived (sales ÷ share; cumulative sales) exactly as documented in the paper.
